# First Bayesian Identifiability Experiment

> **Superseded numerical detail:** This exploratory run evaluated normal
> transition densities at 5 mm bin centers. The later replicate-design
> experiment integrates normal probabilities over target bins exactly and
> shows that the apparent recruit-variance bias below was partly a quadrature
> artifact. See `docs/replicate-process-first-run.md`. The broader conclusions
> about precision, pseudo-truth, and confounding remain useful.

## Purpose

This experiment asks whether a decomposed inverse IPM can recover known vital
rates from repeated size profiles generated by an individual-based population
simulation.

The simulation does not propagate a deterministic IPM. Individuals survive,
grow, and reproduce through separate stochastic events. The recovery model
ignores identities and uses only annual size-profile counts.

## Experimental Setup

- Initial population: 2500 individuals
- Annual transitions available: 12
- Environment and latent individual quality: held constant
- Observation: complete annual census, binned into 5 mm size intervals
- Fitted transition lengths: 2, 4, 8, and 12 years
- Recovery kernel: positive, decomposed survival-growth and recruitment pieces
- Unknown demographic parameters: 10
- Initial size profile: conditioned upon as known
- Bayesian computation: four adaptive Metropolis chains
- Prior regimes: weak, informative-correct, and informative-wrong

The first likelihood is a deterministic IPM with Poisson profile counts. A
second model introduces positive latent operating intensities:

```text
lambda_tj | mu_tj, phi ~ Gamma(phi, phi / mu_tj)
y_tj | lambda_tj ~ Poisson(lambda_tj)
```

After integrating out `lambda_tj`, this is a Gamma-Poisson or
negative-binomial likelihood.

## Main Results

### Parameter Coverage

| Transitions | Weak Poisson | Informative Poisson | Wrong Informative Poisson | Weak Gamma-Poisson |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.80 | 0.90 | 0.80 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.90 | 0.90 |
| 8 | 0.60 | 0.70 | 0.50 | 0.70 |
| 12 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.60 | 0.60 |

### Derived-Quantity Coverage

| Transitions | Weak Poisson | Weak Gamma-Poisson |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 0.87 | 1.00 |
| 4 | 0.87 | 0.87 |
| 8 | 0.60 | 0.67 |
| 12 | 0.47 | 0.60 |

The decline in coverage with additional years is not evidence that less data
are preferable. It shows that the current recovery likelihood is becoming
increasingly certain around a population-scale pseudo-truth that differs from
the individual-level generating rates.

## What Was Identified

With twelve transitions, the weak-prior Poisson model recovered survival
probabilities accurately across representative sizes:

| Quantity | Truth | Posterior mean |
|---|---:|---:|
| Survival at 50 mm | 0.354 | 0.357 |
| Survival at 80 mm | 0.589 | 0.589 |
| Survival at 110 mm | 0.789 | 0.787 |

Recruit-size means were also accurately recovered. This occurred despite high
posterior correlations among some intercept and slope parameters. Therefore,
coefficient correlation alone should not be interpreted as failure to identify
the vital-rate curve over the observed size range.

## What Was Not Recovered

With twelve transitions, the weak-prior Poisson model produced narrow intervals
that missed the individual-level truth for:

- growth increment at 80 mm;
- recruitment level and recruitment size slope;
- recruit-size standard deviation;
- long-run population growth rate.

For example:

| Quantity | Truth | Posterior mean | 95% interval |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Growth increment at 80 mm | 8.400 | 8.054 | 7.772, 8.338 |
| Recruitment at 80 mm | 0.184 | 0.234 | 0.204, 0.261 |
| Recruit-size SD | 3.500 | 3.718 | 3.661, 3.777 |
| Population growth `lambda` | 1.029 | 1.022 | 1.019, 1.024 |

The Gamma-Poisson model widened uncertainty and improved derived-quantity
coverage, but retained the main biases. This indicates that independent
overdispersion cannot represent the structured process differences across size
and time.

## Confounding Patterns

The strongest posterior correlations were approximately:

- `-0.99` between recruitment level and recruitment slope for short series;
- `-0.97` between recruit mean and its parent-size slope for longer series.

These pairs can produce nearly equivalent profiles over the sizes that
contribute most to the observed population. Reporting curves and
likelihood-informed combinations is therefore more useful than reporting
coefficient marginals alone.

## Prior Sensitivity

Moderately informative incorrect priors affected the two-transition analysis
and worsened mixing. By four transitions, the likelihood largely overwhelmed
the imposed prior shifts. However, this did not guarantee recovery of
individual-level truth: all prior regimes converged toward similar biased
population-scale estimates for longer series.

Strong agreement across priors is therefore not sufficient evidence of correct
vital-rate identification.

## Computational Diagnostics

Most fits had:

- chain acceptance rates near 0.20 to 0.28;
- maximum split R-hat below 1.04;
- minimum approximate effective sample size above 190.

The two-transition Gamma-Poisson and wrong-informative-prior fits mixed poorly.
That poor mixing is consistent with weak identification, but those particular
posterior summaries require longer or better-targeted sampling before formal
use.

## Interpretation

This first experiment supports three important methodological claims:

1. Repeated profiles can identify some vital-rate curves and demographic
   functionals.
2. More years can sharply increase precision without recovering
   individual-level biological truth.
3. A process-error model must represent correlated changes in profile shape,
   not merely independent overdispersion.

## Required Next Experiments

1. Simulate multiple replicate populations under the same vital rates.
2. Compare one long series with many shorter replicate series.
3. Add size-correlated latent process discrepancy.
4. Estimate population-effective truth separately from individual-level truth.
5. Fit mark-recapture and integrated models to the same simulations.
6. Repeat the experiment across population sizes and profile sample sizes.
7. Use simulation-based calibration when data are generated exactly from the
   fitted hierarchical model.
