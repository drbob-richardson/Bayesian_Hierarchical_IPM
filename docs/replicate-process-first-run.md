# Replicate Populations and Correlated Process Discrepancy

## Questions

This experiment asks:

1. With a fixed total of twelve annual transitions, is one long trajectory or
   a collection of shorter independent trajectories more informative?
2. Does independent Gamma-Poisson overdispersion restore honest uncertainty?
3. Does a positive size-correlated process discrepancy improve recovery?

## Designs

Three designs use the same total number of transitions:

| Design | Populations | Transitions per population |
|---|---:|---:|
| `one_by_twelve` | 1 | 12 |
| `three_by_four` | 3 | 4 |
| `six_by_two` | 6 | 2 |

Each population begins with 2500 independently generated individuals and uses
the same individual-level vital rates. Annual profiles are complete censuses
binned into 5 mm size intervals.

Equal transition count does not imply exactly equal total observed fish. The
realized totals were approximately 31,500, 31,200, and 37,100 fish,
respectively.

Each design was fitted with:

- deterministic IPM plus Poisson profile likelihood;
- deterministic IPM plus Gamma-Poisson profile likelihood;
- positive low-rank log-Gaussian process discrepancy over size.

## Important Numerical Finding

The original numerical kernel evaluated conditional normal densities only at
bin centers. For a true recruit-size SD of 3.5 mm and 5 mm observation bins,
this produced an apparent but artificial recruit-variance bias.

The corrected kernel computes:

```text
Pr(lower_j < X <= upper_j)
  = Phi(upper_j) - Phi(lower_j)
```

for every target-size bin. After this correction, recruit-size variance was
recovered by the ordinary models.

Numerical integration and observation-bin construction are therefore part of
the inferential problem, not merely implementation details.

## Corrected Results

### Parameter Coverage

| Design | Poisson | Gamma-Poisson | Correlated process |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `one_by_twelve` | 0.70 | 0.70 | 0.90* |
| `three_by_four` | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 |
| `six_by_two` | 0.80 | 0.80 | 0.80 |

### Derived-Quantity Coverage

| Design | Poisson | Gamma-Poisson | Correlated process |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `one_by_twelve` | 0.73 | 0.73 | 0.93* |
| `three_by_four` | 0.67 | 0.67 | 0.67 |
| `six_by_two` | 0.87 | 0.87 | 0.87 |

`*` The one-long-trajectory correlated-process fit did not converge. Its
maximum split R-hat was approximately 2.10, so its apparent coverage
improvement cannot be treated as a valid result.

All other corrected fits had maximum split R-hat below approximately 1.04.

## Design-Specific Confounding

No design uniformly dominated. Instead, each design exposed different weak
directions.

### One Long Trajectory

The ordinary models underestimated recruitment across representative sizes and
slightly underestimated population growth. More observations from the same
realized population trajectory did not average away that trajectory's
particular recruitment history.

### Three Four-Transition Trajectories

The ordinary models overestimated growth increment and growth variance. They
also overestimated recruitment by large individuals and population growth.

### Six Two-Transition Trajectories

The ordinary models recovered most demographic quantities but overestimated
recruitment at small and medium sizes. With only two transitions per
population, recruitment level and slope remained strongly confounded.

This suggests a design tradeoff:

- independent populations help average over population-level realizations;
- longer within-population trajectories help distinguish transition
  mechanisms;
- very short trajectories retain structural confounding even when replicated.

One simulated dataset per design is not enough to determine the optimal
balance. A Monte Carlo design study is required.

## Process Models

### Independent Gamma-Poisson

The Gamma-Poisson model produced almost the same coverage and posterior
locations as the Poisson model. Independent bin-level overdispersion did not
capture the structured discrepancies generated by cohorts moving through size.

### Correlated Log-Gaussian Process

The correlated model applies positive smooth multiplicative deviations to each
projected profile and propagates them into later profiles.

For the replicated designs, posterior median process SDs were small:

| Design | Posterior median process SD |
|---|---:|
| `three_by_four` | 0.008 |
| `six_by_two` | 0.021 |

The correlated model converged for these designs but did not materially improve
coverage. For the one-long-trajectory design it exposed a difficult
demographic/process ridge and failed to converge. That ridge is scientifically
interesting, but the current random-walk sampler is inadequate for final
inference.

## Conclusions From This Run

1. Replication is useful, but "more populations" is not a complete design rule.
2. Very short replicated trajectories can still leave survival-growth and
   recruitment components confounded.
3. Independent overdispersion is not an adequate process model.
4. Flexible correlated process error can itself become confounded with vital
   rates.
5. Correct integration over observed size bins is essential.
6. Monte Carlo replication is needed before making study-design claims.

## Next Required Work

1. Repeat each design across many independently simulated datasets.
2. Compare designs at both equal transition count and equal total survey cost.
3. Cache trajectory-specific likelihood pieces and move the correlated model to
   HMC, NIMBLE, or TMB.
4. Add cohort-pulse and environmental-excitation designs.
5. Add mark-recapture and oracle benchmarks for survival and growth.
6. Evaluate a restricted discrepancy model orthogonal to demographic
   sensitivity directions.
