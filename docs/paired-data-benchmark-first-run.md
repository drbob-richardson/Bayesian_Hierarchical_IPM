# Paired Profile and Individual-Data Benchmark

## Question

What is learned from repeated size profiles, capture-recapture transitions, and
their integration when all data streams arise from the same individual-based
fish populations?

This benchmark is deliberately harder than validating an inverse IPM using
data generated by the fitted IPM. Fish survive, grow, and reproduce
individually. The recovery models see only the data assigned to each analysis.

## Design

The experiment contained 200 independently simulated datasets. Each dataset
contained:

- three replicate populations;
- four annual transitions per population;
- 500 fish in each initial population;
- 25% detection in annual size profiles; and
- 35% known detection in annual capture-recapture samples.

Across datasets, the average observed data volume was:

| Data component | Mean per dataset |
|---|---:|
| Fish observed in size profiles | 1,499 |
| Capture records | 2,100 |
| Captured fish eligible for a next-year recapture | 1,682 |
| Consecutive recaptures | 272 |

Six analyses were fitted to each dataset:

1. Poisson profile-only inverse IPM;
2. Gamma-Poisson profile-only inverse IPM;
3. capture-recapture transitions only;
4. Poisson integrated profile and capture-recapture model;
5. Gamma-Poisson integrated profile and capture-recapture model; and
6. complete-history oracle.

The capture-recapture likelihood conditions on fish captured in year \(t\).
Recapture in year \(t+1\) has probability equal to size-specific survival
multiplied by known detection. Growth is observed for consecutive recaptures.
Consequently, capture-recapture directly informs survival and growth but
contains no reproductive or parent-offspring information.

The oracle observes all individual survival outcomes, growth transitions,
offspring counts including zeros, parent-offspring links, and recruit sizes.

## Computational Performance

All 1,200 fits completed. Of their Laplace posterior Hessians, 1,191
(99.25%) were positive definite. Approximation failures occurred only in
profile-only analyses:

| Analysis | Positive-definite fits |
|---|---:|
| Poisson profiles | 198 / 200 |
| Gamma-Poisson profiles | 193 / 200 |
| Capture-recapture | 200 / 200 |
| Poisson integrated | 200 / 200 |
| Gamma-Poisson integrated | 200 / 200 |
| Complete-history oracle | 200 / 200 |

Non-positive-definite approximations were retained as diagnostic failures but
excluded from scientific summaries.

Capture-recapture recruitment coverage of one is not evidence of recovery.
Recruitment and recruit-size parameters contracted by less than 1% from their
weak priors in the capture-recapture analysis. Its recruitment and population
growth intervals were extremely wide.

## Interpretation

### Identities efficiently identify survival and growth

Capture-recapture reduced growth error substantially relative to profile-only
analyses while retaining near-nominal coverage. This establishes a clear
benchmark for what is lost by discarding identities.

### Profiles contain information unavailable to capture-recapture

Profiles strongly contracted recruitment and recruit-size parameters.
Capture-recapture alone did not. However, profile information about
recruitment remained poorly calibrated: recruitment-at-80 coverage was only
0.49 under Poisson profiles and 0.57 under Gamma-Poisson profiles.

### More information can worsen calibration under misspecification

Integrated fits improved survival point accuracy relative to either data stream
alone, but their intervals became too narrow. Survival-at-80 coverage fell to
0.80 under Poisson integration and 0.85 under Gamma-Poisson integration,
despite lower RMSE.

The likely explanation is incompatibility between the individual-based
population generator and the profile likelihood. Accurate individual
transition information anchors survival and growth. Remaining discrepancies
between observed and deterministically projected profiles must then be
absorbed by other demographic parameters, particularly recruitment. Combining
likelihoods increases apparent information without resolving that model
discrepancy.

### Gamma-Poisson overdispersion helps but is insufficient

Gamma-Poisson profiles generally improved coverage for survival, recruitment,
recruit size, and population growth. In the integrated analysis, it raised
survival coverage from 0.80 to 0.85 and recruitment coverage from 0.51 to
0.60. Independent bin-level overdispersion did not restore nominal coverage,
indicating that the remaining discrepancy is structured across sizes and
years.

## Publication-Level Contribution

The benchmark supports a stronger paper claim:

> Repeated size profiles contain real information about individual vital
> rates, but their demographic decomposition can be weakly identified and
> sensitive to process-model misspecification. Adding accurate individual data
> improves point estimation yet can increase false certainty when profile
> discrepancies are treated as independent Poisson variation.

This connects inverse-IPM identifiability, integrated-data analysis, and model
misspecification. It also shows why reporting prior contraction, accuracy, and
coverage together is essential.

## Important Limitations

- Capture detection was known and constant.
- The capture-recapture analysis used consecutive recaptures rather than a
  full latent-alive-state encounter-history model.
- Data streams were treated as conditionally independent in the integrated
  likelihood.
- The recovery kernel was time-homogeneous while the finite individual
  population generated structured demographic fluctuations.
- Scientific summaries used Laplace approximations; representative integrated
  fits still require full MCMC audits.

## Highest-Priority Next Test

The next methodological experiment should replace independent Gamma-Poisson
overdispersion with a structured profile process model, or use modular/robust
integration that prevents a misspecified profile likelihood from
overwhelming well-calibrated individual transition information. A full
capture-recapture model with unknown detection should then replace the current
known-detection benchmark.
